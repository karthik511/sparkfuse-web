The reference build
Built for a garment exporter whose orders arrived five different ways.

Purchase orders and amendments from three buyer portals, tech packs as email attachments and transfer links, factory invoices on WhatsApp. Every one was opened, read and re-typed into a sheet — and the ones nobody opened became the late shipments. Now intake watches every channel, reads the document, creates or updates the order, and raises only the ones that need a decision: a date moved, a price changed, a quantity that doesn't match.

Questions accounts teams ask.

How accurate is it?
On clean PDFs, above 98% per field after tuning; on photos, lower — which is why every field carries a confidence score and anything below your threshold is held, not posted.
What if the supplier's format changes?
It reads meaning, not positions, so a new layout usually just works; if a field is missed, it lands in the exception queue and the correction teaches it.
Does our data leave India?
Processing runs in an Indian region; documents stay in your account and are not used to train anything shared.
Can it do GST and e-invoice checks?
It verifies GSTIN, tax arithmetic and IRN presence; filing itself stays with your CA or Tally.
Who approves exceptions?
Whoever you name, by threshold — a ₹2,000 rate difference to the buyer, a ₹2 L one to the owner. Every decision is logged.
We're not a factory. Does this apply?
Yes — the same intake handles booking confirmations for stays, dealer orders, clinic bills. The document types change; the method doesn't.
