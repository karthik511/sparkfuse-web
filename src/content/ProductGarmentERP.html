The reference build
Our deepest reference: 200+ live orders, three buyers, twelve factories, one system.

Time & action plans lived in Excel, one file per order; sampling updates were missed until the buyer asked; status was buried in threads and portals; nobody could say what the order book was worth today. The build read the buyers' portals and inboxes in, generated a T&A for every order, gave sampling and pricing their own clocks, put inspections on a phone and gave the owner a live order book — with an AI layer that plans, assigns, reads and flags.

Questions merchandisers ask.

Our buyers each have a different portal and PO format.
That's the fitted 30%. Each buyer's portal and format is mapped in discovery; amendments arrive as changes to the order, not as new documents to re-read.
What happens when a stage is skipped?
It shows as overdue until someone logs it — deliberately. Skipped stages are how orders go quiet, so the system nudges rather than hides.
Can factories see buyer prices?
No. Buyer documents are sanitised before factories see them — names, prices and logos removed — and factory quotes are kept apart from buyer prices.
Do inspectors need laptops?
A phone. In-line, mid-line, pre-final and final checks with AQL sampling, defects and photos, and the report generated and emailed from the app.
Does it handle combos and colourways?
Yes — one production plan per combo inside a PO, sharing the ETD and shipment.
We're a factory, not a buying house. Does it fit?
The T&A, inspections and sampling modules do; the sourcing and buyer-pricing side is dropped. That's the manufacturing ERP page.
