The reference build
Built for a protective-products manufacturer in Pune. Two days on site, fourteen people, nine sessions — then the system.

Seven departments, seventeen people on full-time data entry, and a buying team spending a third of its week chasing rather than buying. The build started with the process — what to automate, what to leave alone, which number to move — and only then the modules: inventory and material traceability first, customer and vendor portals, planning and dashboards, cost estimation added at the owner's instruction.

Questions plant owners ask.

We already bought an ERP and nobody uses it. Why would this be different?
Because the process gets redesigned first and the software is built around it — then rolled out by the people who built it, on your floor, until your team runs it. Software that bends the team to it is what gets abandoned.
Does it replace Tally?
No. Tally stays your books; this runs the floor and posts to Tally. Same for an existing SAP or Busy setup.
Our data is in twenty spreadsheets. Can it be migrated?
Yes — that's most of week 3. Sheets become the backup, not the system.
Can it start with one module?
Yes. Inventory & traceability is usually first because it finds money on day one. Every phase is usable alone.
We have one IT person. Is that enough?
Yes. It's built to be light — cloud-hosted, no servers on site, your IT person trained to administer users and reports.
How is the AI kept from making a wrong call?
It reads, compiles, warns and drafts. Anything that changes stock, price or a plan asks a person first — until you decide, per action, that it may act alone.
