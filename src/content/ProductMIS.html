The reference build
"Ultimately we want the software to be a catching tool." Four layers, drawn by the owner himself.

A multi-outlet restaurant group had every report format already — checklists, scorecards, wastage logs, complaint registers — and no way to get data in, compile it, or act on it without a person in the middle. The strategy followed the owner's own four layers: bringing data in (phones, POS, cameras), compiling it (automatically, nightly), presenting it (one screen per role, a digest per morning), and governance (rules that ask a named person a question with a deadline).

Questions owners ask.

We already have Power BI. Is this different?
Power BI presents; this brings the data in and acts on it. It can feed your Power BI, or replace it with role screens — your choice in discovery.
Our data is on paper checklists.
Then the first module is the phone checklist that replaces them — same questions, same format, photos where you want proof.
Won't managers feel policed?
The question goes to one person, privately, with the data attached. Most managers prefer that to a group WhatsApp from the owner.
Can it read CCTV?
For counts and presence — footfall, staff at station, kitchen activity — on your existing cameras. Details on the Vision AI page.
How many rules is too many?
Start with five. Every rule has an owner and a deadline; if nobody would act on it, it isn't a rule, it's a report.
Does it work for a factory or a sales team?
Yes — the same four layers, different data sources. This page's example is restaurants because that's where the owner drew it.
